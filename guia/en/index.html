How it works

One workshop, 6 workbenches, and 8 parts

Think of the hangar as a workshop. Each stage happens at a workbench called a platform, and each robot part stands for one of your assistant’s abilities. The event calls this assistant an agent: an AI that can do tasks as well as answer questions. The terminal is the computer window where you paste commands. Linux is an operating system, like Windows and macOS, and Ubuntu is one version of it.

01

Learn how an agent works

Run an agent with free AI on your computer and see how it uses tools.

Needs: a computer and a terminal. The course uses Python, a programming language. The AI is free and runs on your computer.
02

Build your own, safely

Your assistant remembers you and works in a separate copy without access to your passwords.

Needs: a computer, a terminal, and copying commands, without writing code. On a regular computer, with no payment, it remembers what you ask, but can do very few tasks. To do more, use a good graphics card or paid AI in the cloud. The separate copy uses Docker, a program that keeps the assistant isolated. Course tested on Ubuntu.
03

Connect it to Telegram

You talk to a Jarvis that recognizes one owner and follows the rules you wrote.

Needs: a computer to set it up. After that, you can chat on your phone. If you followed Your Safe Jarvis, this is lesson 5. It was tested with a simulated Telegram, so the real connection may need some adjustments.
04

Give it a voice

You talk by voice. It listens and answers on your computer.

Needs: a powerful computer: a Mac with an Apple chip and 16 GB of memory or more, or a PC with an NVIDIA graphics card with 12 GB or more. Setup uses Claude Code or Codex, coding assistants. The stage guide has details.
05

See everything on one screen

A dashboard shows your computer’s automatic routines and anything still pending, without changing anything.

Needs: a computer with Python. The automatic routines are read from Linux.
06

Help it improve every week

The problem log shows what to adjust, and the numbers help you decide what it can do on its own.

Needs: an assistant that is already set up, so you can keep an eye on it.

You don’t have to do everything. The courses for each stage are independent, and some build an agent of their own. Choose a path and follow it. In Your Safe Jarvis, lesson 5 shows you how to connect the assistant to your phone through Telegram.

The 8 parts, in everyday words

🗣️ Where it speaks

Terminal, Telegram, voice, or screen.

💡 Which model thinks

The AI that answers. You can switch it without rebuilding the assistant.

🪪 Who it is

A file with its identity and rules.

🧠 What it remembers

What carries over from one conversation to the next.

🖐️ What it does

Each tool allows an action and has limits on how it can be used.

🔒 What it cannot do

A restricted folder with no passwords. Risky actions need your OK.

✅ How it checks its work

It tests what it did before saying it is done.

📓 How it improves

A log of what went wrong, reviewed each week.

⚠️ What the event does not promise

  • The hangar robot represents your progress. The real assistant comes from tasks you do on your computer.
  • Marking a stage as done does not check your work. You check it yourself using the “done when” item on each platform.
  • It is an open page with no date and no live stream. Go at your own pace.
How to start

Choose your starting point

You don’t need to install anything to enter the event. If you like, enter a name for your badge, then choose between the 3D hangar and the version without 3D. Then follow the path that suits you.

🖥️ First, check your computer

Windows or macOS: start with the tour. The no-code path has not yet been tested on these systems.
Linux with a powerful graphics card: you can follow the full no-code path.
Linux without a powerful graphics card: the assistant will remember, but can do very few tasks unless you pay for AI in the cloud, a service accessed over the internet.

👀 I just want to look around

Go in, walk around the platforms, and watch the videos. It works on a computer or phone, and you don’t have to do any tasks.

🧩 I want to build it without coding

Go straight to stage 2 with the Your Safe Jarvis course. You don’t write code, but you do need to open the terminal and copy commands. There are 9 lessons, about 2h20, plus installation time. Lessons 6 and 7 use Docker.

📱 I want to get to Telegram

The Agent, Piece by Piece course builds an agent of its own. It is the most technical path: it requires a terminal, Python, git, and Node.js, and works on Linux, macOS, and on Windows through WSL, Linux inside Windows. The AI comes from your ChatGPT subscription through Codex, or from a program installed on your computer.

💻 I want to understand how it works inside

Start at stage 1 with the Agent System from Scratch course, which runs an agent in Python, a programming language.

Common questions

Before you join

How much does it cost?

The event, courses, and guides are free. In Your Safe Jarvis, the AI on your computer is free, while cloud AI costs money. The Agent, Piece by Piece course uses your ChatGPT subscription or an AI installed on your computer, and the voice stage uses Claude Code or Codex during setup. These services have their own rules and plans, so check yours before you start.

Do I need to sign up?

No. The badge name is optional, and your progress is saved in your browser.

Does it work on a phone?

You can visit using touch controls or the version without 3D. To build the assistant, you need a computer.

What if the 3D does not open?

The event switches to the version without 3D, with the same schedule and progress.

Do I need to know how to code?

No, not to visit. To build it without writing code, follow Your Safe Jarvis. You will need to open the terminal and copy commands. Two lessons use Docker, a program that keeps the assistant isolated.

Can my computer handle it?

It depends on the stage, and the voice stage needs the most power. Your Safe Jarvis was tested on Ubuntu. It has not been tested on macOS in practice. On Windows, it depends on WSL, Linux inside Windows, and that combination has not been tested either.

Can I talk to it on my phone?

Yes, after setup, with your computer on and the assistant running. In Your Safe Jarvis, Telegram is lesson 5. It was tested with a simulated Telegram, so the real connection may need some adjustments. Agent, Piece by Piece is another path.

Doesn’t ChatGPT already have memory?

It has its own memory features. Here, you build an assistant with rules you write yourself. It can do tasks on your computer and asks for your OK before taking a risky action.

Does the event build the assistant for me?

No. It lays out the path, and you do the tasks. The hangar robot only shows your progress.
